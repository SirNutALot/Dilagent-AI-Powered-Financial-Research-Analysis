                  How Dilagent works

                  Three pillars, then a combined verdict

                  Analytics scores a company on three separate passes, then combines them. The percentages are confidence in each pillar under Dilagent’s rules, not a predicted return.

                  • Fundamentals — published ratios and statements from Yahoo Finance, plus optional notes pulled from an SEC 10-K/10-Q or an uploaded PDF.
                  • Technicals — price-history measures (RSI, MACD, SMA 20/50/200, ATR, 52-week range) computed from Yahoo daily history. Used as entry timing, not business quality.
                  • News / external — Yahoo ticker news plus RSS headlines, scanned for controversy keywords. This is a language screen, not a full legal review.

                  A quality score is 88% fundamentals and 12% news. A timing score is the technical score alone. The headline Dilagent score is a weighted blend of all three pillars. Horizon changes the blend; it does not forecast a future price.

                  How confidence scores are calculated

                  The weights this app actually uses

                  Combined score = weighted mean of the three pillar scores:

                  HorizonFundamentalsTechnicalsNews
                  Up to 10 days38%32%30%
                  Up to 60 days50%25%25%
                  Up to 1 year62%16%22%
                  Beyond 1 year70%10%20%

                  Fundamentals themselves are a weighted mean of: profitability 28%, growth 20%, balance sheet 20%, cash 14%, valuation 12%, and optional filing notes 6%. Each sub-score is a banded mapping of published ratios (for example ROE, margins, revenue growth, debt/equity, P/E), not a neural net.

                  Confidence (Low / Medium / High) is a coverage check: whether fundamentals, a 200-day average, and headlines all arrived, how far the pillars disagree, and whether news controversy is severe. It is not a probability of profit.

                  Verdict labels (Investable, Cautious, Not investable) are rule thresholds on quality, timing, and headline vetoes — for example a keyword hit for bankruptcy or fraud can force “Not investable” regardless of the numeric blend.

                  Language model

                  What an LLM does and does not do

                  If a Groq or OpenAI key is present, Dilagent may ask llama-3.3-70b-versatile (Groq) or gpt-4o-mini (OpenAI) to write the short briefing paragraph only. The model receives the already-computed scores, ratings, and highlights. It does not set pillar scores, does not choose the verdict, and does not invent numbers. If no key is configured, Dilagent writes a template briefing from those same scores. Discover, Watchlist, and the numeric cards never call the model.

                  Data sources

                  Where the numbers come from

                  • SEC EDGAR — company tickers and 10-K / 10-Q HTML when a CIK exists.
                  • Yahoo Finance (via yfinance) — prices, statements, and the published ratios used in fundamentals and Discover.
                  • NSE EQUITY_L.csv — official NSE equity list for Indian names, mapped to Yahoo .NS tickers.
                  • News RSS — Financial Times, Reuters Business, CNBC, MarketWatch, BBC Business, Yahoo Finance, plus Google News and Yahoo ticker stories.
                  • Your PDF — an uploaded annual report is indexed locally and can add filing notes to the fundamental score.

                  Discover screens only Yahoo profile fields Dilagent has actually fetched. Dilagent scores appear on Discover and Watchlist only after an Analytics run in this browser. Fields Dilagent does not store (for example RSI on the Discover screen, or ROCE without a statements pull) show as not available rather than an estimate.

                  Limitations

                  What this tool cannot promise

                  • Feeds can be delayed, incomplete, or wrong.
                  • Company facts change after the last pull.
                  • Headline keyword tags can misread context.
                  • Ratios are accounting snapshots, not forecasts.
                  • Past growth and prices do not guarantee future results.
                  • The combined verdict can be wrong.
